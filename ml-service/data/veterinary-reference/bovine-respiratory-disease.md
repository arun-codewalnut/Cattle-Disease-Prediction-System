# Bovine Respiratory Disease (BRD)

Bovine Respiratory Disease is a general term for respiratory illness in cattle, usually
caused by a combination of viral and bacterial pathogens taking advantage of stress or
crowded conditions — such as transport, weaning, mixing of animals from different
sources, or poor ventilation. It's one of the most common causes of illness in young and
newly-arrived cattle.

Typical signs include fever, nasal discharge, coughing, and labored or rapid breathing.
Affected animals often appear depressed, reduce their feed intake, and may stand with
their head lowered and ears drooping. In more severe cases, breathing becomes visibly
difficult, with animals breathing through an open mouth.

Management usually involves reducing stress (good ventilation, minimizing overcrowding
and abrupt mixing), and antibiotic treatment for the bacterial component under veterinary
guidance, since early treatment significantly improves outcomes. Prevention focuses on
vaccination programs and good management practices around weaning and transport. BRD is
common and generally managed at the individual-animal or herd level rather than treated
as a reportable disease the way FMD or LSD are.
