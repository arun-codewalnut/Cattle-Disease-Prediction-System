# Fungal Infection

Fungal skin infections in dogs are most often ringworm (despite the name, a fungus, not a
worm) or a yeast (*Malassezia*) overgrowth. Both thrive in warm, moist skin folds or areas
that stay damp, and ringworm specifically is contagious — it can spread between animals and
to people through direct contact or contaminated bedding/grooming tools.

Signs include circular patches of hair loss with scaly or crusty skin (classic for
ringworm), or greasy, itchy, odorous skin with darkened patches (more typical of yeast).
Both can look similar to other skin conditions, which is why lab confirmation matters more
here than for most skin issues.

This is a general educational summary for a learning project, not veterinary guidance —
see the project's disclaimer.

## Precautions

- If ringworm is suspected, limit contact between the affected dog and other pets or children until a vet confirms the cause — it's contagious.
- Wash bedding, brushes, and any surfaces the dog has contacted, since fungal spores can persist in the environment.
- Avoid sharing grooming tools between animals until the infection has cleared.

## Next steps

- Consult a veterinarian for confirmation (fungal cultures or a skin scraping) — fungal and bacterial infections can look alike, and treatment (antifungal vs. antibiotic) differs.
- Follow the full course of any prescribed antifungal treatment, even after visible signs improve, since fungal infections often relapse if treatment stops early.
- Monitor other pets and household members for similar skin changes given ringworm's contagious nature.
