# Hypersensitivity/Allergic Dermatosis

Hypersensitivity (allergic) dermatosis is skin inflammation driven by an overactive immune
response rather than an infection — common triggers include flea saliva (flea allergy
dermatitis), food ingredients, or environmental allergens (pollen, dust mites, mold). Unlike
a bacterial or fungal infection, there's no single organism causing it, which is why it
often needs identifying and managing a trigger rather than a single course of treatment.

Signs include intense itching, redness, chronic licking or chewing (especially paws, belly,
and ears), recurrent ear infections, and skin that thickens or darkens over time from
repeated scratching. Symptoms are often seasonal (environmental allergens) or
year-round (food, fleas).

This is a general educational summary for a learning project, not veterinary guidance —
see the project's disclaimer.

## Precautions

- Keep up flea prevention year-round, since flea allergy is one of the most common and most preventable triggers.
- Avoid introducing new foods or treats until a possible food allergy has been ruled out or confirmed with a vet.
- Discourage scratching/chewing where possible (an e-collar during flare-ups) to prevent secondary skin infections.

## Next steps

- Consult a veterinarian to help identify the trigger — this can involve a flea-control trial, a food elimination trial, or allergy testing, since treatment is more effective once the cause is known.
- Ask about anti-itch or immune-modulating treatment options for symptom relief while the underlying trigger is being identified.
- Watch for signs of secondary infection (worsening redness, odor, discharge) from repeated scratching, which would need separate treatment.
