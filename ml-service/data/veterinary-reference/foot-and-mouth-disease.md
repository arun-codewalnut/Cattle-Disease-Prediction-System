# Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)

Foot and Mouth Disease is a highly contagious viral disease affecting cloven-hooved
animals, including cattle, pigs, sheep, and goats. It spreads rapidly through direct
contact between animals, contaminated equipment, vehicles, or people moving between farms,
and can also travel short distances through the air under the right conditions.

Typical signs in cattle include a sudden fever, drooling or excessive salivation, and
painful blister-like lesions (vesicles) on the tongue, gums, muzzle, and around the
hooves. These lesions can rupture and cause lameness, reluctance to eat, and a noticeable
drop in milk production. Young animals are more vulnerable to severe complications.

Because FMD spreads so quickly and can devastate livestock economies, it is classified as
a notifiable/reportable disease in most countries — suspected cases must be reported to
animal health authorities immediately, and affected animals are typically quarantined
while confirmatory testing takes place. There is no specific cure; management focuses on
strict biosecurity, movement restriction, and supportive care under veterinary guidance.
