# Lumpy Skin Disease (LSD)

Lumpy Skin Disease is a viral disease of cattle and water buffalo, spread mainly by
biting insects such as certain flies, mosquitoes, and ticks, rather than by direct
nose-to-nose contact. Because of this, outbreaks often follow patterns tied to insect
activity and warmer, wetter seasons.

Affected cattle typically develop a fever followed by firm, round skin nodules (a few
centimeters across) scattered over the body, especially on the head, neck, legs, and
udder. The nodules can ulcerate and become sites for secondary infection or fly-strike.
Animals often show reduced appetite, a drop in milk yield, swelling of the limbs, and
enlarged lymph nodes. In pregnant cows, infection can sometimes lead to abortion.

Like FMD, Lumpy Skin Disease is considered a notifiable/reportable disease in many
regions because of its economic impact and tendency to spread across herds and borders.
Management centers on vector control (reducing biting-insect exposure), vaccination
programs where available, movement restrictions, and supportive veterinary care —
there's no specific antiviral cure.

see the project's disclaimer.

## Precautions

- Isolate the affected animal and reduce its exposure to biting insects (fly/tick control) to limit further spread.
- Restrict movement of animals, people, and equipment on and off the farm.
- Avoid moving unaffected animals near affected ones until the situation is assessed.

## Next steps

- Contact your veterinarian or local animal health authority immediately — this is a reportable disease and must not be handled without official involvement.
- Ask about vaccination options for the rest of the herd where available.
- Follow any quarantine or biosecurity instructions given by animal health authorities.
