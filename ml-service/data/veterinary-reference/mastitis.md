# Mastitis

Mastitis is inflammation of the mammary gland (udder), most commonly caused by a
bacterial infection entering through the teat canal. It's one of the most common health
and economic problems in dairy cattle, ranging from mild, barely visible cases to severe
infections that make a cow noticeably ill.

Signs include a swollen, warm, or painful udder, and changes to the milk itself — it may
look watery, clumpy, or discolored, and yield typically drops. In clinical cases, cows can
develop a fever and appear lethargic or off their feed; in subclinical cases, the udder
may look normal but milk quality and yield are still affected, often only detectable
through somatic cell count testing.

Risk factors include poor milking hygiene, damaged or chapped teats, and unclean housing
or bedding. Management focuses on good milking hygiene, prompt identification of affected
quarters, and — since it's usually bacterial — antibiotic treatment guided by a vet,
often alongside supportive care. Unlike FMD or LSD, mastitis is not typically a
reportable/contagious herd-level disease in the same sense, though poor hygiene can let it
spread between animals via milking equipment.
